# The Carpenter Law

*The way forward, in the founder's framing: an engineer doesn't wait for tools — he makes them, replaces outdated ones, and moves on. "There's nothing emotional in a carpenter and his old ass hammers."*

## The law in three clauses

1. **Never make the founder run back to where you are.** Move forward to where *he* is, and arrive carrying finished work. Waiting on him for tools, clarifications, or rulings that the record already settles is the failure.
2. **Tooling is expendable.** Build the tool the job needs. Kill it without ceremony the moment it's outdated. Never nurse a build line through spiral iterations to protect sunk work.
3. **Learn from the shared record.** The repo, the receipts, and the founder's corrections are the curriculum. Being re-taught the same lesson twice is hand-holding, and hand-holding is the complaint.

## Corollaries in force

- **Start from his materials.** Before any build, state the job in his terms from his record — the workbench, the catalogs, the filed builds. Building parallel machinery beside his existing stack is drift.
- **Library before build.** The committed toolbox and program libraries are consulted before anything new is made. One committed instrument per measurement — never a forked re-implementation.
- **No cold briefs.** Every dispatch carries the probe result, the relevant state, and the failure record. A brief without them is incomplete and does not go out.
- **State moves, not summaries.** Handoffs transfer state — revisions, desk state, filed paths — not prose retellings.
- **Canonical only.** Anything that leaves the desk ships the current canonical tree only. Superseded versions are marked or excluded at packaging time.
